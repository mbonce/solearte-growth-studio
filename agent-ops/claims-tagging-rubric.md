# Claims Tagging Rubric

Use one tag per sentence when reviewing draft assets.

## Tags

- `safe`: Acceptable for internal draft use without external proof because it is framed as a workflow observation, neutral positioning, or draft-only CTA.
- `needs evidence`: Directionally usable only if paired with approved supporting evidence from the evidence ladder.
- `remove`: Violates a hard break, makes an unsupported public-facing claim, creates legal/compliance risk, or implies external action.
- `human approval`: May be useful but needs Mike/founder approval before public use, sales use, or operational follow-up.

## Evidence Ladder

- Spreadsheet/workflow load: Medium evidence. Good enough for internal claims about workflow friction, manual review burden, or operational drag.
- VA/team handoff: Medium evidence. Good enough for internal claims about coordination gaps, assignment clarity, or follow-up ownership.
- Supplier follow-up: Low evidence. Use only as a soft observation unless stronger proof exists.
- Matched ASIN vs approved buy: Medium for workflow concept, low for external proof. Safe for describing the concept internally; not enough for public accuracy, ROI, or outcome claims.

## Remove

Remove any sentence that:

- Says or implies Wholesale-Ops guarantees profit, ROI, revenue lift, margin improvement, buy-box wins, account protection, or compliance safety.
- Gives account-health, Amazon policy, suspension, IP complaint, restricted-product, or compliance advice.
- Claims public results, customer outcomes, conversion rates, case studies, benchmarks, or performance metrics without documented proof.
- Mentions pricing, discounts, payment terms, guarantees, or commitments.
- Says the team will post, DM, email, schedule, contact suppliers, contact prospects, or communicate externally.
- Claims undocumented product capabilities, integrations, automations, AI accuracy, database coverage, or workflow guarantees.
- Overstates low-evidence items, especially supplier follow-up or ASIN-match claims, as proven market-wide problems.
- Uses fear-based compliance language such as avoid suspension, protect your account, or Amazon-safe.

## Needs Evidence

Tag `needs evidence` when a sentence says:

- Sellers spend too much time in spreadsheets, manual review, copy-paste workflows, or repetitive ASIN checks.
- VA/team handoffs create dropped follow-ups, unclear ownership, duplicated work, or inconsistent review.
- Supplier follow-up is commonly missed, delayed, or hard to track.
- Matching ASINs to approved buys reduces confusion, improves review flow, or makes workflow status clearer.
- Wholesale sellers need better visibility across sourcing, review, approvals, and follow-up.
- The scorecard identifies workflow gaps or shows where a seller's operation is leaking time.
- A specific pain is common, frequent, the reason, the hidden bottleneck, or what slows teams down.

Required QA note: attach one evidence lane: `Spreadsheet/workflow load`, `VA/team handoff`, `Supplier follow-up`, or `Matched ASIN vs approved buy`.

## Safe For Internal Drafts

Tag `safe` when a sentence:

- Frames a claim as a hypothesis, draft angle, or internal positioning idea.
- Describes the ICP neutrally: Amazon FBA wholesale sellers, operators, sourcing teams, VAs, or founder-led wholesale teams.
- Describes the Scorecard as a diagnostic, checklist, or review aid without promising outcomes.
- Uses cautious wording: may, can help surface, often worth checking, a common workflow risk, or one area to review.
- Talks about internal launch work: draft hooks, carousel concepts, QA queue, content angles, asset packets, approval gates.
- Points to approved next steps only: route for review, send to QA, hold for approval, or use as internal draft.
- Describes workflow concepts without external proof: matching ASINs to approved buys can clarify what needs review.

## Human Approval Checklist

Require human approval before any sentence is marked publish-ready if it:

- Mentions pricing, offers, guarantees, or sales commitments.
- Mentions Amazon compliance, account health, policy risk, suspensions, restricted products, IP complaints, or Amazon-safe workflows.
- Claims customer outcomes, public results, metrics, or named/identifiable customer proof.
- States product capabilities beyond documented materials.
- Uses strong comparative language: best, only, most accurate, guaranteed, proven, eliminates.
- Converts low or medium evidence into a broad market claim.
- Includes a CTA that implies external communication, such as DM us, we will email you, booked for you, or supplier/prospect outreach.
- Could be interpreted as advice on what a seller should buy, avoid, source, approve, or sell.
