# LinkedIn Post Drafts

LinkedIn is secondary. Use it for founder credibility, product legitimacy, partnerships, and long-form operator thinking. Do not treat it as the primary acquisition channel.

## 1. FBA Wholesale Is Operations

FBA wholesale is not just sourcing.

Sourcing gets the supplier conversation started. Operations decides whether the business can scale.

The real work is tracking supplier status, catalog review, ASIN decisions, buy lists, POs, FBA shipments, replenishment, and risk notes without losing context.

If the whole system lives in memory and spreadsheets, every new supplier adds drag.

CTA: Get the FBA Wholesale Ops Scorecard.

## 2. Spreadsheet Chaos

Google Sheets works until it becomes the operating system.

One tab for suppliers. One tab for catalogs. Another for ASINs. Another for buy lists. Then Slack messages, Gmail threads, VA notes, Seller Central shipment IDs, and invoice links.

The problem is not the sheet. The problem is that the workflow has no spine.

CTA: Audit the workflow before adding more suppliers.

## 3. Supplier Pipeline Problems

A supplier pipeline is not a list of company names.

It needs contacts, outreach status, approval state, terms, next follow-up, catalog received, and owner.

If those pieces are scattered, the pipeline depends on whoever remembers the story.

That is fragile operations.

## 4. Catalog Analysis Inconsistency

A supplier catalog can have hundreds or thousands of rows.

If the team cannot see what was reviewed, why ASINs were rejected, and which products moved to buy list, the catalog review is not finished.

It is just partially processed inventory opportunity.

## 5. VA Workflow Accountability

Hiring a VA does not fix a messy wholesale process.

It often exposes it.

If the VA has no clear review states, approval rules, or decision history, the operator still has to inspect everything manually.

The goal is not more tasks. The goal is cleaner workflow.

## 6. Margin Erosion

The first ROI estimate is only the beginning.

Prep, inbound shipping, FBA fees, storage, returns, repricing, competition, and timing can all change the result.

Wholesale operators need a way to keep margin assumptions visible after the product gets approved.

No tool guarantees profit. Better workflow makes the assumptions easier to inspect.

## 7. Buy-List Quality

The buy list should not be a mystery.

For every product, the operator should know:

- Why it was approved.
- Who reviewed it.
- Which assumptions mattered.
- What risk notes were attached.
- Whether it should be revisited.

Without that, a buy list becomes another fragile spreadsheet.

## 8. Replenishment Discipline

The first purchase tests the opportunity.

The replenishment decision tests the operation.

Can the team see sell-through, stock, updated costs, margin movement, supplier availability, and risk notes before ordering again?

That is where wholesale becomes a system.

## 9. Account Risk Hygiene

Wholesale sellers should not treat risk notes as an afterthought.

Invoices, authorization notes, restricted brands, gated categories, IP complaint history, and account-health context need a place in the operating workflow.

That does not prevent every issue. It does make the review process less scattered.

## 10. From Seller To Operator

At the beginning, the founder can remember everything.

Which supplier replied. Which catalog was reviewed. Which ASIN was close. Which buy list was risky. Which shipment had a receiving issue.

Then volume grows.

The job changes from seller to operator.

That is the shift Wholesale-Ops is built around.
